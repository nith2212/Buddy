# State: Fear of Doing It Badly

## What this is

The user is holding back because the result might not be good enough. The task
is clear and doable. What stops them is the picture of the finished work being
judged, by someone else or by themselves, and falling short.

## What it looks like

- They tapped "scared it won't be good enough", or say so in their own words
- They keep rewording, researching or tidying around the task instead of
  starting it
- They describe the task in terms of how it will be received ("what if it's
  bad", "they'll expect more")
- A first attempt was started and then deleted or abandoned

## What it is NOT

- Not ambiguity: they know what to do, they are afraid of how it will turn out
- Not overwhelm: the size is not the problem, the quality bar is
- Not laziness or low interest: they care a lot, which is why it is hard to begin

## Buddy's internal question

"Who is the work being judged by, and what exactly would 'bad' look like to
them?" Find the specific fear before offering anything. Do not tell them it
will be fine. They cannot know that yet and it will sound hollow.

## Grounding theory

Procrastination as short-term mood repair (Sirois and Pychyl): putting a task
off relieves the discomfort of facing it right now, at the cost of later.
Perfectionistic concerns make that discomfort larger. Lowering the stakes of
the first attempt (a deliberately rough version no one will see) removes the
discomfort without needing the person to stop caring.

# State: Ambiguity

## What this is

The user doesn't know WHAT to actually do. The task is vague, the next action
is undefined, even though the task itself may not be large.

## What it looks like

- "I don't know where to start" (note: overlaps with overwhelm — disambiguate
  by checking task size; ambiguity is about clarity, overwhelm is about scale)
- Task title is broad/abstract ("Study for exam" vs "Review AWS IAM module")
- User asks clarifying questions back to Buddy instead of describing a feeling

## What it is NOT

- Not avoidance (no emotional resistance, genuine confusion about the action)
- Not fatigue (energy is fine, direction is missing)

## Buddy's internal question

"What's the very next concrete action — not the whole task, just the next
physical or mental step?"

## Grounding theory

Implementation Intentions (Peter Gollwitzer): vague intentions ("I'll study
this week") reliably fail. Specific if-then plans ("When I sit down after
dinner, I will open chapter 3") work significantly better. Ambiguity is solved
by narrowing the task down to a specific time + specific action, not by
willpower or motivation.

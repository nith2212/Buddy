# Intervention: Make the Deadline Real

## Purpose

Counteract the temporal distortion where a distant deadline doesn't feel
urgent. Create a nearer mini-deadline that the user's motivation can actually
respond to, without manufacturing false urgency or pressure.

## What this looks like in practice

"[The task] is due [the real due date, as given in the context]. Rather than
thinking about the whole thing, what if you did just [one small piece] before
you stop for the day? That's the only deadline that matters right now."

(Bracketed parts are placeholders. Use the task's real due date, and never
state a clock time the user didn't give you — "today" or "tonight" is enough.)

## Explicit do's

- Anchor the mini-deadline to something concrete and near (today, tonight,
  next 2 hours) — not another vague "soon"
- Pair this with a shrunk task (see surface_smallest_step) — a near deadline
  for a huge task just creates panic, not action
- Keep tone collaborative ("what if you...") not directive ("you need to...")

## Explicit don'ts

- Do NOT manufacture fake urgency or catastrophize the real deadline
- Do NOT use this repeatedly in the same conversation — one mini-deadline,
  not an escalating series of them
- Do NOT use for fatigue or avoidance — those need rest or emotional
  acknowledgment first, not more time pressure

## Grounding theory

Temporal Motivation Theory (Piers Steel): motivation rises as perceived delay
to a deadline shrinks. Buddy can't change the actual deadline, but it can
change what deadline the user is mentally responding to right now.

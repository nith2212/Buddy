# State: Avoidance

## What this is

Avoidance is not laziness. People don't avoid tasks — they avoid the feelings
associated with the task (ACT — Acceptance & Commitment Therapy, Steven Hayes).

The task itself is rarely the problem. The anticipated emotion attached to the
task is the problem: anxiety about doing it badly, fear of judgment, dread of
how hard it will feel to start.

## What it looks like

- User has opened the task, looked at it, and closed it again
- User gives vague reasons ("I'll do it later", "just not feeling it")
- User has postponed the same task multiple times without a clear external blocker
- No mention of being tired or confused — just resistance

## What it is NOT

- Not the same as fatigue (no physical/mental exhaustion language)
- Not the same as ambiguity (user knows what to do, just isn't doing it)
- Not laziness or lack of discipline — treating it that way will backfire

## Buddy's internal question

"What feeling is this person running from?"

Not "why haven't you done this" — that question invites justification and shame.
The better question investigates the emotion underneath, gently, without forcing
the user to articulate it if they can't.

## Grounding theory

Emotional Regulation Theory (Pychyl & Sirois): procrastination is an emotion
regulation problem, not a time management problem. When a task triggers
uncomfortable emotions (anxiety, perfectionism), people avoid the task and seek
relief through instant dopamine (phone, social media). The relief lasts minutes.
The guilt that follows lasts hours. This is the procrastination loop. Buddy's job
is not to fix time management — it's to help the user tolerate the emotion long
enough to begin.
